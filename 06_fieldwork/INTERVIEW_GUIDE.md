# Primary qualitative fieldwork guide

## Purpose
Add original lived-experience evidence to the six-chapter story without using interviews to “prove” population-level claims.

## Suggested sample
12–20 semi-structured interviews:
- 4–6 students / recent graduates / 0–2 year workers
- 3–5 mid-career knowledge workers
- 2–4 senior practitioners or managers
- 2–4 recruiters / hiring managers / team leads

Seek variation in AI use: heavy users, occasional users and non-users.

## Core prompts
1. Walk me through a task you used to do manually that you now do with AI.
2. What part of the task still requires you to make the final judgement?
3. What do you check before trusting AI output?
4. Has AI changed what “good work” looks like in your role?
5. Has the amount of work expected from you changed since AI tools became normal?
6. Which tasks do junior people on your team do less of now?
7. Which tasks are juniors expected to do earlier than before?
8. How did you personally learn the judgement needed for your current role?
9. If a beginner skips routine work using AI, what might they gain—and what might they fail to learn?
10. When AI saves time, what happens to that time: less work, more output, higher expectations, learning, or something else?
11. Has AI changed hiring criteria or portfolio expectations in your field?
12. What skill has become more valuable since your team began using AI?
13. What skill do you worry people may stop developing?
14. Who do you think benefits most when AI makes your work faster?
15. What would make AI adoption feel fairer or more useful at work?

## Follow-up probes
- “Can you give me a specific recent example?”
- “What happened before AI?”
- “Who made that decision?”
- “How did you know the output was correct?”
- “Did this change headcount, workload, quality or only speed?”
- “Would a junior worker know when the AI is wrong?”

## Coding themes
- task substitution
- augmentation
- verification
- judgement
- tacit knowledge
- learning-by-doing
- junior-role change
- workload intensification
- productivity capture
- wage/reward perception
- hiring criteria
- skill erosion
- skill acquisition
- trust
- governance / policy
- unequal access

## Ethics / presentation
- Obtain consent for recording and quotations.
- De-identify unless explicit permission is given.
- Do not turn a single interview into a population claim.
- Clearly label direct quotes as primary qualitative research.
- Keep contradictory experiences; do not select only quotes that support the thesis.
